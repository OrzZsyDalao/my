\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CLASP compares network-transition and feasible-corridor distributions over the
same traceroute segments. Across the existing one-hour RIPE Atlas snapshot,
projection-score Layer Agreement is 0.632 for 67 island and archipelagic
service-facing units and 0.001 for 202 coastal-mainland units; the observed
group difference remains in the same direction under equal-share and Top-1
allocation. The service-facing population is not uniformly contractive:
81 of 275 units are network-broad/corridor-concentrated, while 158 remain broad
at both layers. These results show that network-layer diversity and feasible
physical-corridor diversity have a geographic and population-specific
relationship in the measured snapshot.
