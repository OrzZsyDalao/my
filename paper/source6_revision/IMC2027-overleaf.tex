\title{When Network Diversity Narrows at Sea:\\
A Cross-Layer View of Internet Service Paths}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Network-layer path diversity is often used to describe the alternatives
available to an Internet service, but traceroute does not reveal whether those
alternatives occupy distinct physical directions. We present CLASP, a paired
cross-layer audit that constructs network-transition and feasible
landing-region-corridor distributions from the same traceroute segments. We
apply CLASP to 490,911 valid IPv4 traceroutes from 18 public RIPE Atlas
measurements covering 13 DNS Roots, three applications, and two dynamic
multi-target references. Under projection-score weighting, the Spearman rank
association between network and corridor Top-2 concentration is 0.632 across
67 island and archipelagic service-facing units and 0.001 across 202 coastal-
mainland units. The observed difference remains in the same direction under
equal-share and Top-1 candidate allocation, where the corresponding gaps are
0.461 and 0.715. The relationship is heterogeneous within the service-facing
population: 81 of 275 units move from a broad network distribution to a
concentrated corridor distribution, while 158 remain broad at both layers.
These measurements show that the correspondence between network and feasible
physical-corridor diversity varies across geographic settings and cannot be
inferred from network-layer breadth alone.
\end{abstract}
