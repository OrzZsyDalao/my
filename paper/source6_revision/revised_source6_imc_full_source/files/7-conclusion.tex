\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CLASP compares network-transition and feasible-corridor distributions over
the same traceroute segments. In the aligned one-hour RIPE Atlas snapshot,
81 of 275 service-facing units have broad network support and concentrated
corridor support under equal-share allocation, while effective category
count contracts in 204 units (74.2\%). Across units, projection-score
Layer Agreement is 0.632 for 67 island and archipelagic units and 0.001 for
202 coastal-mainland units. The geographic ordering persists under
equal-share and Top-1 allocation, with between-group differences of
0.461, 0.631, and 0.715 across the three rules. Network-layer diversity
audits therefore need to examine both within-unit physical-corridor
concentration and the geographic variation in cross-layer rank association.
