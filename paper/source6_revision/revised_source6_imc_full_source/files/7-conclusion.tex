\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

CLASP compares network-transition and feasible-corridor distributions over the
same traceroute segments. In the aligned one-hour RIPE Atlas snapshot,
projection-score Layer Agreement is 0.632 for 67 island and archipelagic
service-facing units and 0.001 for 202 coastal-mainland units. The same
geographic ordering remains under equal-share and Top-1 allocation, with
between-group differences of 0.461, 0.631, and 0.715. Network-layer diversity
therefore provides a much stronger signal of feasible physical-corridor
diversity for island and archipelagic sources than for coastal-mainland
sources. Cross-layer audits should account for this geographic difference
before treating network diversity as evidence of physically distinct service
paths.
