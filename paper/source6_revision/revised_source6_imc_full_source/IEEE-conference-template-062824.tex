\title{When Does Network Diversity Reflect Physical Diversity?\\
A Cross-Layer Audit of Internet Service Paths}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Author(s)}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Network-layer path diversity is often used as a proxy for the physical
diversity supporting Internet services, but traceroute does not identify the
submarine corridors that could carry an observed path. We present CLASP, a
Cross-Layer Audit of Service Paths that constructs network-transition and
feasible-corridor distributions from the same traceroute segments. We apply
CLASP to 490,911 valid IPv4 traceroutes from 18 public RIPE Atlas measurements
covering all 13 DNS Roots, three applications, and two dynamic multi-target
topology references. The resulting audit contains 370 country--measurement
units. Under projection-score weighting, the Spearman association between
network and corridor concentration, which we call Layer Agreement, is 0.632
for 67 island and archipelagic service-facing units and 0.001 for 202 coastal-
mainland units. The geographic difference remains in the same direction under
equal-share and Top-1 candidate allocation, with differences of 0.461, 0.631,
and 0.715. The service-facing population is heterogeneous: 81 of 275 units
have broad network support and concentrated corridor support, while 158 remain
broad at both layers. These measurements show that the correspondence between
network-layer and feasible physical-corridor diversity varies across the
observed geographic settings; they do not establish a causal geographic
mechanism or ground-truth cable use.
\end{abstract}
